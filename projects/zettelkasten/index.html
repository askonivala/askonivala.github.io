Zettelkasten: The History of Card Indexes and Analog Databases 1773–1998

November 10, 2026

PI: Asko Nivala

The German word Zettelkasten refers to a paper-based index card system stored in drawers, which scholars and artists used to organise their notes. Before the widespread adoption of punch cards and computers, card indexes were the most common technology available for maintaining databases.

Zettelkasten: The History of Card Indexes and Analog Databases 1773–1998 (The Kone Foundation 2027–2029) investigates paper-based card indexes and other analog databases used by scholars and writers from the 1770s to the 1990s. The project addresses the following research questions:

  1. How was knowledge in the humanities collected and organised into databases before the advent of computers?
  2. How can originally paper-based card indexes be studied using digital methods?
  3. What are the distinctive characteristics of card indexes as a medium compared to books and notebooks?
  4. What can today’s students and researchers learn from pre-digital methods of collecting and classifying information?

These research questions shed light on the historical development of analog methods of information management and their pedagogical significance for the humanities and literary studies.

The zettelkasten has been a particularly popular medium in German-speaking Europe but I will collect sources from other countries as well. Some of the card indexes are available in digital form or have been compiled into books, while others require archival research. I will conduct the research through case studies selected from the eighteenth to the twentieth centuries. The results of the three-year project will be published as an English-language monograph.

I will also analyse originally paper-based card indexes using digital methods, such as network analysis. This approach may reveal previously unrecognised patterns and connections in historical materials, including aspects that even the original creators of the collections were unable to perceive.

The project aims to uncover the forgotten history of the card index and, in doing so, introduce contemporary students and researchers to practices that enabled efficient and systematic note-taking long before computers became widespread.
